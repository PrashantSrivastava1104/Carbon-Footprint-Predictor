The model

XGBoost, trained on 10,000 synthetic records

We compared three models — Linear Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost — on an 80/20 train/test split with a fixed random seed. The scaler and encoder are fitted on training data only, inside an sklearn Pipeline, so there is no data leakage. XGBoost won on every metric.

Dataset

Individual Carbon Footprint Calculation

Author: Mesut Duman · Source: Kaggle · Licence: CC0 Public Domain.
10,000 rows, 20 columns. Target: yearly kg CO₂e divided by 12 to give monthly values.

This dataset is synthetically generated. The records are not from real surveys — they were produced from a deterministic formula. This is why the XGBoost model achieves R² = 0.9721: it essentially re-learns the formula. On real survey data with messy human responses, expect R² of 0.5–0.8.

Known limitations

Be aware of these before citing this tool's output.

  • Formula-derived target. The near-perfect R² is an artefact of the synthetic data generation, not a sign of real-world accuracy.
  • No geographic adaptation. Emission factors vary by country and grid mix. This model has no region input.
  • Multi-selects simplified. Recycling and cooking appliances are encoded as counts (how many), not which ones. Some signal is lost.
  • Static tips. Tips are rule-based, not ranked by SHAP values for your specific prediction.
  • Currency conversion is approximate. Grocery bill is converted at ₹83 = $1. This rate changes over time.

Leakage prevention

The train/test split happens before any preprocessing. StandardScaler and OneHotEncoder are fitted on training data only, inside an sklearn Pipeline. Label thresholds use training-set quantiles only.

To retrain

With the virtual environment active, run: 

python src/train.py

This regenerates all model artifacts, metrics, and the feature importance chart.
